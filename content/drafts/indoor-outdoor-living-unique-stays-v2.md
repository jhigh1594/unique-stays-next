There is a particular kind of disorientation that happens in certain places. You step through the threshold and something does not resolve quite right. Light arrives from directions it should not. One wall is stone: raw canyon stone, not veneer. Another wall is glass. A third is open air. You put your bag down and realize: this is a room, technically. But the boundary is not where you expected it.

The category of indoor-outdoor stays spans wider than screened porch cabins or sunroom rentals, though it includes those. It covers cliff houses where the rock face is structural and not decorative. Skylight domes where the ceiling is a window. Glamping tents and geodesic structures where the only material separating you from the weather is fabric or curved glass. Houseboats where the yard does not exist in the conventional sense because the deck is floating above water. Outdoor showers placed deliberately outside as an architectural statement. Circular yurts where the geometry dissolves into a lake horizon.

Indoor-outdoor living in a vacation rental, at its best, is an architectural decision about the relationship between shelter and landscape. Not a deck attached to a house. A position on a question: how open should a home be to the place it occupies?

We looked through our inventory for properties built around that question. Eight came back with clear, distinct answers. All different.

---

## What Makes a Stay Truly Indoor-Outdoor

"Indoor-outdoor living" in a vacation rental means architectural design that intentionally merges interior and exterior space. A screened porch cabin is one version: the mesh keeps the insects out but lets the evening air, the sound of the rain, and the edge of the landscape in. A sunroom is another: glass added to the fourth wall turns the surrounding scenery into the dominant presence in the room. At the more extreme end, you have properties where the exterior is structural, and the ceiling is the sky.

The eight stays below each represent a different answer to that design question. The price range runs from $177 to $519 per night. The regions run from the Ozark highlands to the Sonoran Desert to the Pacific Northwest canopy. What they share is a specific architectural decision about what it means to be inside.

## The Cliff That Became a Room

[EMBED: sage-canyon-cliff-house-co]

One wall is red rock. Not stone facing, not stone veneer: the cliff itself, running from the floor of the room up into the canyon above, unadorned and unfinished by anyone but erosion. Colorado's most-wishlisted Airbnb is built into the side of McElmo Canyon, 40 minutes west of Mesa Verde National Park.

The house has a loft bedroom, a full kitchen, and a yard that occupies a separate rock alcove nearby. Petroglyphs are visible from the property. Canyon views carry for miles down the valley, with Sleeping Ute Mountain on the horizon. The indoor-outdoor relationship here is not decorative. The outside is load-bearing. You can feel the logic of it the moment you walk in.

Colorado's Most-Wishlisted designation comes from Airbnb's 2025 data. At $250/night, 4.98 stars across 372 reviews.

## The Ceiling That Became the Sky

[EMBED: romantic-mountain-dome-nc]

The skylight is positioned directly above the bed. This is not incidental; it is the reason to go. Near Sylva, in the foothills of the Smoky Mountains in North Carolina, the dome is small and quiet and the view upward is the whole point.

You lie flat on your back in the same orientation you would take at a proper planetarium. The creek runs below the property, audible through the dome walls. The private hot tub sits a few steps outside, for the contrast of warm water against cool mountain air. This is what a sunroom becomes when you remove the floor from under it and put the sky directly overhead.

$225/night, 4.99 stars across 236 reviews on Airbnb. Harrah's Cherokee Casino is 20 minutes away; the town of Sylva is 10.

## The Bathroom That Moved Outside

[EMBED: skydome-hideaway-tx]

On a hill among post oak trees west of Dallas, the first luxury geodesic dome in the DFW area has a hot tub. It also has an outdoor shower. That second amenity tells you something about the design philosophy of the place: the person who built this was thinking about the line between private function and exterior space and decided it was worth challenging.

An outdoor shower is not an oversight. It is a choice. It means the most intimate part of the daily routine now involves the sky, whatever the sky is doing that morning. At the SkyDome Hideaway, that sky is the Texas sky: wide, sometimes theatrical, reliably present. The dome provides enclosure when you want it. The shower gives you back to the weather when you do not.

$275/night, 4.98 stars, 429 reviews on Airbnb. DFW is under an hour east.

## The Walls That Slide Open

[EMBED: treehouse-oregon]

A 42-inch old-growth Douglas fir grows up through the floor, up through the room, and up into the canopy above. The Osprey Treehouse in Stevenson, Washington, is a 20-foot octagon engineered around that tree's presence. The fir is not a decorative element. The building was designed to exist alongside it.

What makes this an indoor-outdoor property, specifically, is the glass and the door. Oversized windows on every wall, plus a sliding door on the canopy-facing side that opens the octagon entirely to the Pacific Northwest air. When that door is back, the treehouse becomes a platform. The fir is there. The Columbia River Gorge light is there. The structure stops asserting itself and lets the landscape in.

Sleeps two adults. $519/night, 4.94 stars, 156 reviews on Airbnb. Accessible via the Historic Columbia River Highway, about an hour east of Portland.

## The Deck That Outgrew the Room

[EMBED: 87-getaway-treehouse-ar]

The wrap-around deck circles the cabin on all sides. Sit in any direction and you are looking into the hardwood ridges of the Arkansas Ozarks. The interior is substantial: a king bed, a jacuzzi tub, a fireplace. But the deck is the living room. That is where this treehouse near Mountain View, Arkansas, happens.

Mountain View is the folk music capital of the Ozarks, which is a genuinely specific designation. The town holds public jam sessions in the courthouse square on most Friday and Saturday evenings. The Ozark Folk Center State Park maintains living demonstrations of traditional Appalachian music and craft. Buffalo National River, one of the cleaner float rivers in the country, is a short drive north.

$177/night, 4.91 stars across 98 reviews on VRBO. The most accessible price point in this roundup.

## The Yard That Became Water

[EMBED: houseboat-nomad-pemaquid-me]

The yard does not exist. Below the wrap deck: lake. The pine forest that rings Lake Pemaquid stays on the shore. What you have on NOMAD is the sense of being enclosed on the water, not of it, the deck suspended above the surface with the lake below and the sky above and nothing in between that belongs to land.

Maine in summer has its own quality: light that arrives early and stays long, the genuine quiet of a freshwater lake that is not a tourist destination, the understanding that the season is happening now and will not wait. Lake Pemaquid is calm enough to kayak in the mornings. The nearest town is Bremen, small and unhurried. Two bedrooms. 4.88 stars across 172 reviews on Airbnb. $275/night.

## The Earth That Became the House

[EMBED: kiva-cave-creek-az]

In Cave Creek, Arizona, the ground is the ceiling, and also three of the walls. The Kiva is an earth-sheltered circular home built in the tradition of Pueblo kiva architecture, staying naturally cool against Arizona heat because the earth itself is the insulation. The design logic is ancient. This is a considered modern interpretation of it.

The effect is not claustrophobic. Handcrafted interior details, saguaro views from the entry, the particular silence of the Sonoran Desert: these register as belonging to a specific place and lineage rather than as enclosure. Kiva architecture is one of the oldest forms of indoor-outdoor integration in North America, a screened porch cabin of a different kind: the screen is stone, and what it shelters you from is the desert sun.

Cave Creek sits north of Phoenix with a strong Western character: restaurants, galleries, and Tonto National Forest are all within reach. $225/night, 4.88 stars across 195 reviews on Airbnb.

## The Horizon That Became the Wall

[EMBED: luxury-yurt-conyers-ga]

The deck faces a private lake and there are no corners to interrupt the view. The circular geometry of a yurt matters here: angles would break the sweep. Instead, the deck extends the curve outward toward the water, and the horizon arrives without a frame.

Thirty miles east of Atlanta, on 80 secluded acres near Conyers, Georgia, this luxury yurt has a canoe, hiking trails through the full 80 acres, and a fireplace for the cooler months. The point is the combination of proximity and actual wildness: you are on private land with a private lake, and Atlanta is 45 minutes away if you need it. For travelers who want the experience of outdoor glamping with the practicality of a metro-area drive, this property understands that equation precisely.

$266/night, 4.93 stars across 88 reviews on VRBO.

## When to Book These Indoor-Outdoor Stays

Season matters more for this category than for most, because the outdoor elements are central rather than supplementary.

The Sage Canyon Cliff House is best in spring and fall. McElmo Canyon summers reach 95 degrees, and the cliff provides only partial shade. The Kiva at Cave Creek is an October-through-April property; Arizona's off-peak temperatures in the 60s and 70s are when the desert silence is an asset rather than a liability. The Osprey Treehouse is accessible year-round, with Pacific Northwest summers (June through September) offering the driest conditions and the most time with the sliding door open.

The skylight dome in North Carolina performs well across most of the calendar, with the best light in spring wildflower season (late April through May) and fall color season (October into early November). The SkyDome Hideaway in Texas is best from October through May; summer heat in the DFW corridor makes outdoor showers a logistical challenge rather than a pleasure. NOMAD on Lake Pemaquid is a June-through-September property.

## FAQ: Indoor-Outdoor Stays, Screened Porch Cabins, and Open-Air Glamping

**What does "indoor-outdoor living" mean in a vacation rental context?**
In a vacation rental, indoor-outdoor living describes architectural design that intentionally merges interior and exterior space. This includes screened porch cabins where a mesh or open wall lets the landscape in without the insects, floor-to-ceiling glass that makes the surrounding environment the dominant visual element, outdoor showers and soaking tubs positioned as primary amenities, earth-sheltered construction, skylight ceilings designed for sleeping under the stars, and structures built around or into natural features like cliffs or trees.

**What is a screened porch cabin and how is it different from having a regular deck?**
A screened porch cabin is a property where the screened or semi-open porch is designed as a primary living space, not added on as an afterthought. The distinction is intentionality: in a true screened porch cabin, the outdoor room is as considered as the interior. The Ozarks treehouse in this roundup is a good example: the wrap-around deck functions as the living room, and the interior is secondary to it.

**Are indoor-outdoor glamping stays comfortable outside of summer?**
It depends on the property's design and the region. Dome properties with proper insulation (the NC skylight dome, the Texas SkyDome) operate comfortably year-round. Earth-sheltered properties like the Cave Creek Kiva are naturally temperature-stable, with spring and fall being ideal. Houseboats are seasonal: NOMAD on Lake Pemaquid is a warm-weather property. Always check the listing for heating and cooling specifications before booking off-season dates.

**What is the price range for indoor-outdoor unique stays?**
The eight stays in this roundup range from $177 to $519 per night. The Arkansas Ozarks treehouse at $177/night and the North Carolina dome at $225/night are the most accessible options. The Osprey Treehouse in Washington at $519/night for two guests is the premium entry, with its price justified by architectural provenance and Columbia River Gorge access. Most properties in this roundup fall between $225 and $300.

**How far in advance should I book a high-design indoor-outdoor stay?**
The Sage Canyon Cliff House, Colorado's most-wishlisted Airbnb, typically books two to three months out for peak dates. The Osprey Treehouse and the NC skylight dome also move quickly. For summer and fall travel, 60 to 90 days in advance is a reasonable baseline. The Ozarks treehouse and the Georgia yurt typically have more availability on shorter notice.
