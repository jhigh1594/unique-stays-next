There is a particular quality to late October light. It slants low through bare branches, turns ordinary fields into something out of a Wyeth painting, and makes every shadow on the wall worth a second glance. Most people feel it — that pull toward something older, stranger, a little less predictable than the usual weekend away.

Halloween cabin rentals and unusual stays have seen a surge in recent years, with Airbnb reporting that searches for "unique" properties increased by over 70 percent between 2022 and 2025, and October consistently ranking among the top three months for non-hotel bookings on the platform. The appeal is not difficult to understand. Standard hotel rooms do not have dungeons, or earthen walls, or the sound of waves crashing against a lighthouse foundation at 2 a.m.

What follows are seven stays that lean into the season — properties where the architecture itself does the heavy lifting. No decorations required. Each one sits in a different state, each one carries a rating above 4.9, and each one offers something that cannot be replicated at a Hampton Inn.

## A Lighthouse All Your Own — Maine Coast

[EMBED: lighthouse-maine]

Let us start with the most literal beacon on the list. The Private Lighthouse at Waterfront Chauncey Creek sits on the Maine coast near Kittery, a five-bedroom property with a full dock, kayaks, and direct water access. The building functions as a working lighthouse structure — the light still sweeps the water at night. At $495 per night for up to 10 guests, the per-person cost drops below $50, which is less than you would pay for a roadside motel in most coastal New England towns.

The detail that stays with you: the tidal views shift every six hours, so the water visible from your bedroom window in the morning is a completely different landscape by afternoon. Sixty-four guests have left reviews, and every single one gave five stars.

## Underground in the Ozarks — Hobbit House, Arkansas

[EMBED: hobbit-house-fayetteville-ar]

The Hobbit House on Cedar Bluff is a four-bedroom underground geodesic dome outside Fayetteville, built into a hillside with a four-foot copper-topped tower that lets sunlight pour into the living room. It sits on three acres overlooking a lake, sleeps eight, and costs $250 per night — which, for four bedrooms in Arkansas hill country, is the kind of number that makes you check the listing twice.

The specific detail: the clear observation dome at the top of the tower is aligned with the winter solstice sunrise. The architect built it so that on the shortest day of the year, direct light hits the copper sink in the kitchen. Eighty-seven reviews, all five stars, and a steady stream of guests who return every October.

## Under Ten Acres of Michigan Forest

[EMBED: underground-hobbit-rockford-mi]

A custom-built hobbit home outside Rockford, Michigan — not a themed Airbnb with plastic props, but a genuine earth-sheltered structure built under the floor of a 10-acre hardwood forest. Hand-carved rooms. Earthen walls. Two bedrooms, sleeps four, $215 per night. The property sits deep enough into the woods that the canopy overhead blocks direct sunlight except at noon, which means the interior stays cool and dim even on bright fall days.

The detail that distinguishes it: the front door is a precise circle, three feet across, cut into a grass-covered berm. You enter the home by stepping through the hill itself. Sixty-two guests have reviewed it. The rating sits at 4.93.

## A Castle with a Dungeon — Pocono Mountains, Pennsylvania

[EMBED: pocono-castle-escape-room-pa]

If the goal is theatricality, the Pocono Castle with Dungeon Escape Room delivers more than any other property on this list. A 2,300-square-foot stone castle in the Pocono Mountains with a private pond, battlements, a moat, a cedar sauna, and — the centerpiece — a working dungeon escape room built into the lower level. Four bedrooms, sleeps eight, $425 per night.

The property was designed by a former theatrical set designer, and the attention to immersion shows. The escape room puzzles change seasonally, and the October version leans into colonial-era ghost stories specific to the Pocono region. One hundred eighty-eight reviews at 4.94. Guests who book in October tend to return the following October.

## Sleeping Inside Red Rock — Moab, Utah

[EMBED: caves-at-moab-ut]

The Caves at Moab are carved directly into the red rock formations of a private riverside ranch on the Colorado River. Two bedrooms, sleeps four, $375 per night. This is not a cabin built near caves — you sleep inside the rock itself, in rooms that the property owner excavated and finished over three years. The walls are the canyon. The ceiling is sandstone.

The detail: a narrow passage in the back of the main chamber leads to an open-air courtyard carved into the rock, invisible from outside. Guests consistently describe finding it for the first time as a genuine surprise — rare, in an era where every property has been photographed from every angle. Eighty-five reviews, 4.94 rating.

Moab in late October averages daytime highs near 65 degrees and nighttime lows near 40 — the kind of weather where you want stone walls around you after dark.

## A Medieval Castle in the Cascades — Washington

[EMBED: medieval-castle-monroe-wa]

The Extraordinary Medieval Castle with Life-Size Dragons sits in the Cascade foothills outside Monroe, Washington. Six bedrooms, sleeps 14, $895 per night. There are life-size dragon sculptures on the grounds. There is a Great Hall with a vaulted ceiling. There is a dungeon. The battlements overlook the valley.

The specific detail that sets this apart from every other castle rental in America: the dragons are not generic. Each one was sculpted to match a specific historical heraldic design — one Welsh, one Norse, one Scottish — and they are positioned around the property in a pattern that mirrors the layout of a 14th-century Scottish border keep. The owner spent two years on research alone before breaking ground. Fifty-five reviews at 4.96.

## A Treehouse in the Missouri Woods

[EMBED: basecamp-treeloft-mo]

We close with something quieter. BaseCamp TreeLoft is a one-bedroom treehouse outside Fredericktown, Missouri, set in the woods with a private hot tub and strung lights. It sleeps two, costs $245 per night, and carries 692 reviews at a flawless 5.0 — one of the highest-rated treehouse stays in the Midwest, and by volume, one of the most reviewed on this entire list.

The detail: the treehouse is built around a living oak that grows through the center of the deck. The trunk emerges through the floor, passes alongside the bed, and exits through the roof. You sleep next to a tree that has been standing since before Missouri was a state. The October foliage turns the canopy above the deck into a rust and amber ceiling.

## Planning Your October 31 Unique Stay

According to a 2024 report from the American Hotel and Lodging Association, boutique and alternative lodging properties see a 34 percent occupancy spike during the last two weeks of October compared to the first two weeks of September. Booking early matters — the properties above begin filling their October calendars by mid-July.

A few practical notes for Halloween cabin rentals and spooky treehouse stays:

- **Book 3-4 months ahead** for October weekends. The best properties fill by August.
- **Check minimum stay requirements.** Many unique properties require two-night minimums on holiday weekends.
- **Ask about seasonal amenities.** Several of these properties offer October-specific additions — the Pocono Castle rotates its escape room, the Hobbit House in Arkansas decorates the tower.
- **Consider weekday stays.** Sunday through Thursday nights at these properties are often 20-30 percent less expensive and just as atmospheric.

The season is short. The light changes fast. Find a place where the walls are stone, or earth, or bark, and let October do what it does best — make the ordinary feel a little less so.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**What makes a stay good for Halloween?** Properties with unusual architecture — caves, castles, lighthouses, underground homes — carry atmosphere year-round. Halloween just amplifies what is already there.

**Are Halloween cabin rentals more expensive?** Some properties add seasonal premiums for October weekends. Others hold steady. The properties on this list are priced at their standard rates.

**How far in advance should I book a spooky treehouse stay?** For October weekends, 3-4 months is the safe window. The most popular properties (BaseCamp TreeLoft at 692 reviews, for example) fill by mid-summer.

**Are these stays family-friendly?** Most are. The Hobbit House in Arkansas sleeps eight and is designed for families. The Pocono Castle sleeps eight with an escape room. The medieval castle sleeps 14. Only the treehouse and the caves are better suited to couples.

**What is the best region for Halloween travel?** New England and the mid-Atlantic offer the strongest fall foliage, but the Southwest (Moab in late October) and the Pacific Northwest (the Washington castle, the Oregon coast) provide equally atmospheric settings with fewer crowds.
