# The Weekly Wanderer: Issue #1 Design

## Overview

First newsletter issue on beehiiv for Unique StaysUSA. "Stay of the Week" format: one curated stay per issue, ~400 words total. Goal: establish voice, create forward-worthy content, set expectations for the series.

**Publication**: Unique Stays USA on beehiiv (`pub_bcf86e50-0eef-4d81-b9fa-3ae42198d7fa`)
**Newsletter name**: The Weekly Wanderer
**Cadence**: Weekly (Mondays)
**Audience**: Experience seekers (design-conscious travelers) + travel dreamers (bucket-list browsers)
**Tier**: Free, no paywall. Audience-building phase.

## Voice & Tone

Witty, confident, slightly addictive. The friend who sends Airbnb links at midnight with "tell me you don't want to live here."

**Tone markers**:
- Opens with a hook that creates "I need to see this" feeling
- Specific and vivid ("a bathtub carved from a single boulder" not "luxurious bathroom")
- Honest about quirks, frames them as part of the appeal
- Plays with rhythm: short punchy sentences, then a longer one that lands like a punchline
- No travel-magazine cliches. No "nestled," "boasting," "picturesque." Ever.
- One emotion per issue. Pick it first, write toward it.
- Frame over hook: how you position the stay matters more than clever wordplay

**Anti-patterns** (from elite-copywriter):
- Zero em dashes. Period, comma, or cut the clause.
- No "Here's why," "Let me show you," "Notice how"
- No "That's X, not Y" comparisons
- No rhetorical question openings ("Have you ever wondered...?")
- No "delve into," "stunning," "unforgettable," "hidden gem"
- No "Here's the thing," "let's be honest," "think of X as Y"
- No artificial section headers (BOTTOM LINE:, CONTEXT:, etc.)

## Issue Structure

| Section | Words | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Hook Chain** | 50-80 | 3-4 sentences. Each opens a loop. Stacked curiosity. Positions the philosophy without naming it. Slippery slope: each sentence makes the next one unavoidable. |
| **The Stay** | 150-200 | Vivid description. What it is, where it is, why it matters. Specific details, honest quirks. The reward for reading the hooks. |
| **The Details** | 30-50 | Quick facts: location, price, sleeps, platform, notable press/features. |
| **CTA** | 1 line | Affiliate booking link. |
| **The Frame** | 20-30 | Close the intro loops. "One stay. Every week. Worth booking." Sets expectation for future issues. |

**Total target**: ~400 words. One hero image.

## Issue #1: The Meadowlark Treehouse

**Stay**: Meadowlark Treehouse, Columbia Falls, Montana
**Emotion**: Awe + "wait, really?" curiosity
**Core hook**: Two living trees grow straight through the interior. Not around. Not beside. Through.

### Hook Chain (~60 words)

Provocative opening about vacation expectations. Stack 3 curiosity loops. Each sentence makes the next one unavoidable. Do not name the newsletter or the stay yet. Let the tension build.

The chain should establish: most people pick boring places to stay. There's a better way. Here's proof.

### The Stay (~180 words)

Meadowlark Treehouse details:
- Two living Douglas firs grow through the floor, through the living space, through the ceiling, and continue their climb above all four exterior decks
- Two-story treehouse in Columbia Falls, Montana
- The space feels less like a building and more like a clearing the trees agreed to share
- Featured on HGTV and in Outside Magazine
- 30 minutes from Glacier National Park
- Vivid sensory details: light through the canopy, the creak of living wood, the smell of cedar
- Honest context: it's remote, you're sharing space with actual trees, that's the point

### The Details (~40 words)

- Location: Columbia Falls, Montana
- Price: $429/night
- Sleeps: 4
- Near: Glacier National Park (30 min)
- Featured: HGTV, Outside Magazine

### CTA

Affiliate booking link to the Meadowlark Treehouse listing.

### The Frame (~25 words)

Close the loops opened in the hook chain. One or two sentences that define what The Weekly Wanderer is. "One stay. Every week. Worth booking." Sets the compact, no-fluff expectation.

### Subject Line Candidates

1. "The trees grow through the floor"
2. "This treehouse has roommates"
3. "Most treehouses are built around trees. This one invited them inside."

Select the strongest option during copywriting.
